To the SIGGRAPH Asia 2026 Art Papers Program Committee,

\bigskip

We submit \textit{Resonating Distance: Staging Proximity and Closure in a Charity Concert for Ukraine} for consideration in the Art Papers track.

The paper documents a charity classical concert held at two venues in Japan in 2026, with fifteen Ukrainian participants joining remotely. Digital elements --- video projection, live audience messaging, and remote connection --- were interpolated into an otherwise classical programme. We examine what that design produced: whether the classical listening experience was sustained under digital addition, and whether a charity concert of this kind generates engagement with a distant conflict or primarily the feeling of having engaged.

\textbf{Why Art Papers, and why SIGGRAPH.} This work belongs to a growing set of practices in which media artists operate within norm-bound performing arts traditions and in direct contact with serious political subjects. The design decisions we document --- what imagery to show, how much to show, when to remain silent, how to frame a war without aestheticizing it --- are decisions that practitioners working at the intersection of media art, performance, and ethics will recognize and face themselves. SIGGRAPH is where those practitioners gather. We believe the paper offers them something concrete: not a success story, but a documented account of what one such attempt produced, including where its structural limits lie.

The community's growing engagement with AI, generative media, and immersive experience has outpaced its engagement with the ethical question of what media art produces when it addresses difficult subjects. We think that question belongs here. It is not being asked in musicology or performance studies, where the question of digital integration remains underexamined. It is not being asked in humanitarian communication, where the design vocabulary is missing. The SIGGRAPH Art Papers track is the venue where both the technical and ethical registers of this practice can be examined together.

\textbf{What the paper claims, and what it does not.} The interpolation worked, with specific caveats: digital media can be introduced into classical concert form without displacing the listening experience, but the calibrations required depend on the audience's prior knowledge of the subject in ways a single design cannot fully anticipate. The ethical finding is more complex. The concert produced genuine proximity and genuine donation. The formal terms that made Ukraine approachable --- restrained imagery, classical structure, reviewed messaging --- were the same terms that made the experience easier to conclude than to continue. We report this not to recommend against the practice but because naming what a practice produces is prior to doing it honestly.

\textbf{Subject matter and representational approach.} The paper was developed in consultation with a Ukrainian partner organization. We follow principles governing the representation of civilian experience that we describe in the paper. The work does not advocate a political position. Its central findings concern design and form. We ask reviewers to assess it on those terms.

We hope this paper contributes to an emerging conversation at SIGGRAPH about what media art practice is responsible for when it takes on subjects of this kind.
