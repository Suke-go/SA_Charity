To the SIGGRAPH Asia 2026 Art Papers Program Committee,

\bigskip

We submit \textit{Resonating Distance} for consideration in the Art Papers track.

The paper documents a charity classical concert held at two venues in Japan in 2026, with
fifteen Ukrainian participants joining remotely. Digital elements were interpolated into an
otherwise classical programme. We examine what that design produced: whether the classical
listening experience was sustained under digital addition, and whether a charity concert of
this kind generates engagement with a distant conflict or primarily the feeling of having
engaged.

We submitted to Art Papers because the design decisions this practice involves --- how to
integrate digital media within a norm-bound performing arts tradition while addressing a
serious political subject --- belong to a conversation the SIGGRAPH community is positioned
to have. Practitioners working at the intersection of media art, performance, and ethics
face exactly these decisions. The Art Papers track is where both the technical and ethical
registers of this kind of work can be examined together, and where a documented account of
what one such attempt produced is most useful.

The findings are in two registers. Digital media can be introduced into classical concert
form without displacing the listening experience, with calibrations that depend on the
audience's prior knowledge of the subject. The ethical finding is more complex: the formal
terms that made Ukraine approachable were the same terms that made the experience easier to
conclude than to continue. We document this because it is the structural condition of this
kind of practice, not a correctable error, and because naming it is prior to doing the
practice honestly.

The paper was developed in consultation with a Ukrainian partner organization. It does not
advocate a political position; its central findings concern design and form. We ask
reviewers to assess it on those terms.
